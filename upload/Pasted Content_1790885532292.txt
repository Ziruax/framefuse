# FINAL IMPLEMENTATION DIRECTIVE

## Professional Kinetic Typography Caption System for 16:9 Storytelling Videos

You are modifying an existing video caption system.

The goal is NOT to simply add more stacked caption layouts.

The goal is to build a **professional, modern kinetic typography system** suitable for 16:9 storytelling, documentary, narrative, emotional, cinematic, and high-retention YouTube videos.

The existing problem is:

> The current "Stack Captions" implementation creates multiple lines of text, but the result still looks like ordinary stacked subtitles. It lacks modern typography hierarchy, composition, semantic emphasis, kinetic movement, visual rhythm, and professional motion-design behavior.

You must fix the underlying system rather than adding superficial animation.

---

# 1. CORE DEFINITION

A **Stack Caption** is NOT:

```text
LINE 1
LINE 2
LINE 3
LINE 4
```

A Stack Caption is a complete **kinetic typography composition**.

Each composition must combine:

* Text segmentation
* Semantic grouping
* Typography hierarchy
* Font hierarchy
* Weight hierarchy
* Scale hierarchy
* Spatial hierarchy
* Alignment
* Word emphasis
* Motion choreography
* Entrance animation
* Emphasis animation
* Hold behavior
* Exit animation
* Timing
* Easing
* Overshoot
* Visual rhythm
* Safe-area management

The final rendered result should still look intentionally designed when paused on a frame.

If the result looks like ordinary subtitles with animation added afterward, the implementation is incorrect.

---

# 2. PRIMARY TARGET

The system is optimized primarily for:

* 16:9 YouTube videos
* 1920x1080
* 1280x720
* Long-form storytelling
* Family storytelling
* Documentary storytelling
* Emotional narratives
* Narrative voiceovers
* Cinematic videos
* High-retention videos

The system must also remain responsive to different resolutions.

Do not hard-code typography exclusively around one canvas size.

Use normalized coordinates, proportional scaling, or an equivalent responsive layout system.

---

# 3. 16:9 CAPTION DENSITY

The current concept uses too few words per composition.

For 16:9 videos, normal kinetic compositions should generally contain:

* 5 to 12 words
* 1 to 3 primary visual lines
* 2 to 5 lines for major dramatic moments
* Approximately 55 to 70 characters as a practical upper boundary for a single composition

Do NOT force every caption into 2 to 4 words.

Short captions are reserved for:

* Major revelations
* Shock moments
* Important keywords
* Emotional climax
* Strong narrative turns
* Single-word impact events

For normal narration, use longer groups.

Example of weak implementation:

```text
I

NEVER

TRUSTED

HIM
```

This is too sparse for normal 16:9 storytelling.

Instead:

```text
I NEVER TRUSTED HIM

BUT I NEVER IMAGINED
HE WOULD LIE TO ME
FOR SO MANY YEARS
```

However, do NOT render these lines identically.

The important words must receive hierarchy.

For example:

```text
I NEVER TRUSTED

        HIM

BUT I NEVER IMAGINED
HE WOULD

        LIE

TO ME FOR SO MANY YEARS
```

The exact composition can vary by preset.

---

# 4. NEVER SHRINK TEXT TO FIT

When text becomes too long:

DO NOT:

* Reduce the font until everything fits
* Make the caption tiny
* Compress all words equally
* Create unreadable blocks

Instead:

1. Segment the narration semantically.
2. Divide it into multiple kinetic compositions.
3. Maintain strong typography.
4. Preserve readability.
5. Use timing to distribute the information.

Example:

Instead of:

```text
I NEVER TRUSTED HIM BUT I NEVER IMAGINED HE WOULD LIE TO ME FOR SO MANY YEARS
```

as one tiny block,

split it:

```text
I NEVER TRUSTED HIM
```

then:

```text
BUT I NEVER IMAGINED
HE WOULD LIE TO ME
```

then:

```text
FOR SO MANY YEARS
```

Each composition should have its own kinetic treatment.

---

# 5. TYPOGRAPHIC HIERARCHY

Every kinetic composition must support hierarchy.

Do not render every word equally.

Hierarchy can be created using:

* Font size
* Font weight
* Font width
* Position
* Tracking
* Opacity
* Scale
* Accent treatment
* Motion intensity
* Duration
* Spatial isolation

Example:

```text
I NEVER

     TRUSTED

HIM
```

`TRUSTED` should be visually dominant.

Another example:

```text
I THOUGHT MY FAMILY
WOULD

       ALWAYS

BE THERE
```

`ALWAYS` becomes the visual anchor.

---

# 6. SEMANTIC EMPHASIS

The engine must understand that not every word deserves equal visual treatment.

Before generating the composition, identify:

* Context words
* Subject
* Action
* Emotional words
* Important nouns
* Important verbs
* Time expressions
* Negations
* Contrasts
* Revelation words
* Narrative keywords

Example narration:

> "I realized my husband had been lying to me for years."

Possible semantic structure:

```text
context:
I realized

subject:
my husband

action:
LYING

duration:
FOR YEARS
```

The typography should reflect this.

Possible result:

```text
I REALIZED

MY HUSBAND

       LYING

FOR YEARS
```

The word `LYING` receives the strongest visual event.

Do not mechanically emphasize random words.

# 7. TYPOGRAPHY PRESET LIBRARY

Create a proper preset library.

Presets must be actual motion typography systems, not merely text positions.

## FAMILY A: CINEMATIC

### 1. Editorial Stack

Sophisticated asymmetric typography.

Use:

* Large primary phrase
* Smaller supporting phrase
* Mixed weights
* Strong whitespace
* Restrained motion

Best for:

* Reflection
* Narration
* Emotional scenes
* Serious storytelling

### 2. Cinematic Stack

Large condensed typography with subtle movement.

Best for:

* Narrative progression
* Documentary storytelling
* Transitional scenes

### 3. Perspective Stack

Use scale and spatial depth.

Words may progressively move:

* toward the viewer
* away from the viewer
* diagonally
* vertically

Best for:

* Memory
* realization
* important narrative moments

### 4. Whisper Type

Small, restrained typography.

Best for:

* intimate narration
* emotional reflection
* quiet scenes

### 5. Progressive Stack

Build the sentence progressively.

Example:

```text
I KNEW

I KNEW SOMETHING

I KNEW SOMETHING WAS WRONG
```

Do not necessarily show all previous text literally. The system may transform or replace earlier phrases.

---

# FAMILY B: DRAMATIC

### 6. Punch Stack

Large stacked typography.

One keyword becomes dominant.

Best for:

* accusations
* emotional statements
* dramatic narration

### 7. Impact Word

One major keyword occupies a large portion of the frame.

Example:

```text
I FINALLY

        UNDERSTOOD
```

`UNDERSTOOD` receives the major animation event.

### 8. Highlight Stack

Normal text plus one dominant keyword.

Best for:

* important facts
* revelations
* narrative emphasis

### 9. Full Screen Type

Typography temporarily becomes the primary visual element.

Use sparingly.

Best for:

* climax
* major revelation
* major narrative turning point

### 10. Build & Collapse

Typography progressively builds and then contracts/disappears.

Best for:

* emotional escalation
* realization
* climax

---

# FAMILY C: CONFLICT

### 11. Split Stack

Separate semantic groups spatially.

Example:

```text
I TRUSTED              HIM

BUT HE

             LIED
```

Different groups may enter from different directions.

### 12. Type Collision

Two text groups move toward one another.

They may:

* collide
* overlap
* push each other
* separate

Best for:

* conflict
* confrontation
* betrayal

### 13. Push Stack

New phrases push previous phrases out of the composition.

Best for:

* escalating arguments
* sequential discoveries

### 14. Opposing Stack

Two semantic groups occupy opposite sides.

Best for:

* contrast
* disagreement
* before/after
* truth vs assumption

### 15. Diagonal Stack

Typography follows a diagonal composition.

Use controlled angles.

Do not rotate text randomly.

---

# FAMILY D: CONVERSATIONAL

### 16. Kinetic Sentence

The sentence remains readable while semantic phrases animate individually.

Example:

```text
I THOUGHT
everything was

        NORMAL
```

Then:

```text
UNTIL
I SAW

        THE MESSAGE
```

### 17. Word Cascade

Words enter sequentially with vertical or horizontal offsets.

### 18. Mixed Weight

Use multiple font weights inside one sentence.

Example:

```text
I WAS
NEVER READY
FOR THIS
```

`NEVER READY` becomes dominant.

### 19. Sliding Sentence

Semantic phrases slide into position sequentially.

### 20. Phrase Reveal

Reveal phrase groups using masks, wipes, clipping, or controlled movement.

---

# FAMILY E: HIGH INTENSITY

### 21. Word Burst

Words enter from multiple controlled spatial directions.

### 22. Collision

Words move toward a common anchor.

### 23. Rapid Stack

Fast sequential typography.

Use only for high-energy moments.

### 24. Full Screen Impact

Very large typography.

Use sparingly.


* revelations
* discoveries
* emotional peaks
* major accusations
* climaxes
* important narrative turns

# 17. STYLE MEMORY

The engine must remember recently used styles.

Maintain:

```text
recentPresets[]
recentFamilies[]
recentAnimations[]
recentLayouts[]
```

Apply repetition penalties.

Rules:

* Do not repeat the exact same preset more than 2 consecutive groups.
* Strongly discourage immediate repetition.
* Avoid repeating the same animation pattern continuously.
* Avoid excessive use of the same typography family.
* Do not repeatedly use full-screen typography.
* Do not repeatedly use impact typography.
* Maintain compatibility between consecutive compositions.

---

# 18. STYLE SELECTION ALGORITHM

Conceptually calculate:

```text
styleScore =
    semanticMatch
  + narrativeMatch
  + captionLengthCompatibility
  + emotionalIntensityMatch
  + visualContinuity
  + userPreference
  + familyCompatibility
  - recentPresetPenalty
  - recentFamilyPenalty
  - animationRepetitionPenalty
  - layoutRepetitionPenalty
```

Select the highest-scoring compatible preset.

Do not use pure randomness.

Controlled variation is required.

# 25. RESPONSIVE SCALING

# 29. RETENTION-ORIENTED VARIATION

The purpose of multi-style typography is to prevent visual monotony.

However, retention must come from:

* narrative progression
* information changes
* semantic emphasis
* visual rhythm
* meaningful typography changes

Do NOT try to maintain retention by constantly adding motion.

The system should create **micro-variation** during normal narration and **macro-variation** during narrative events.

Example:

```text
Normal narration
→ small movement changes

Important statement
→ stronger hierarchy

Suspense
→ progressive movement

Revelation
→ impact composition

Climax
→ major typography event

Reflection
→ visual simplification
```

This creates a natural visual rhythm.

---

# 30. MICRO VS MACRO VARIATION

## MICRO VARIATION

Change:

* alignment
* scale
* stagger
* entrance direction
* word grouping
* weight
* tracking

without completely changing the design language.

Use frequently.

## MACRO VARIATION

Change:

* typography preset
* composition
* typography family
* spatial arrangement
* motion system

Use at meaningful narrative points.

Do not use macro variation every few seconds.

7. Long sentences are shrunk to tiny text.
8. Typography styles are selected randomly.
9. The same preset repeats continuously.
10. Every caption uses maximum animation intensity.
11. Full-screen typography appears constantly.
12. Every word has a bounce or pop animation.
13. Color changes are random.
14. The system ignores semantic meaning.
15. The typography looks generic when paused.
16. Different presets have visually indistinguishable designs.
17. "All Styles" simply randomizes every preset.
18. Auto Mix produces chaotic visual transitions.
19. Manual Mix ignores user weighting.
20. Typography becomes unreadable on 16:9.
21. The system breaks existing caption functionality.
22. Typography variation increases rendering cost unnecessarily.

```text
Editorial / Whisper
```

The viewer should perceive continuous visual movement and variation without feeling that the captions are randomly changing templates.

# 42. FINAL DESIGN PRINCIPLE

The system must follow this hierarchy:

```text
STORY
  ↓
SEMANTIC MEANING
  ↓
NARRATIVE FUNCTION
  ↓
EMOTIONAL INTENSITY
  ↓
TYPOGRAPHY HIERARCHY
  ↓
LAYOUT
  ↓
MOTION
  ↓
VISUAL EFFECTS
```

NOT:

```text
FONT
  ↓
ANIMATION
  ↓
STACK
  ↓
RANDOM EFFECTS
```

The first approach creates professional kinetic typography.

The second creates animated subtitles.

The implementation must follow the first approach.
